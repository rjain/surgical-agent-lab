01

What you will build

A system that audits recorded robotic surgery training sessions. It detects workflow deviations from the dataset’s published annotations, explains them using Gemini’s multimodal reasoning over the relevant video clip, and exposes both through an agent built with the Agent Development Kit.

You write the interesting parts. The data pipeline, the rules engine and the interface are supplied working, so you spend the session on Gemini and ADK rather than on plumbing.

No prior Google Cloud, Gemini or machine-learning experience is assumed. Comfortable intermediate Python is enough: functions, classes, virtual environments, and reading unfamiliar code. No surgical knowledge is needed either — the briefing covers what matters.

You need Python 3.12 or later, the ability to pip install, and Antigravity installed and able to sign in. Antigravity is the environment the lab is built in.

If your laptop is managed and either is blocked, raise an IT ticket today. It is the most common reason someone loses the first half hour, and the one thing that cannot be fixed on the morning.

03

Two logins that are not the same thing

This trips people up, so it is worth thirty seconds now rather than ten minutes on the day.

 AntigravityThe API key
What it isYour own Google sign-in, as normalA string in a file
You use it toOpen and work in the IDELet your code call Gemini
Where it goesThe usual sign-in screenYour local .env, as LAB_GEMINI_API_KEY, nowhere else
The key is not a login

You never sign in anywhere with it, and you will not be given a Google account for this session. Sign into Antigravity with your own account exactly as you normally would. The key only ever goes in .env.

Two things to avoid

  • Do not commit the key. .env is already in .gitignore — just do not move the key out of it.
  • Do not paste the key into an agent chat. Antigravity’s agent can already read your workspace; pasting secrets into a conversation puts them in transcripts and, eventually, commits.
04

On the day

  • Your own key arrives by email shortly before we start. Paste it over the check key in .env — same line, same file, ten seconds. The check key stops working at 13:00, so do this first. If your email has not arrived, say so in the Meet chat and one will be sent to you directly.
  • Bring the laptop you ran the pre-flight on. A different machine means starting step 2 again while everyone else is building.
  • Join the Meet even if you are in the room — the chat is where questions and fixes get posted, and a shared troubleshooting document is linked there.
  • Work in groups of about five. Each group picks one of two applications to build; you will be introduced to both and choose over the break.
  • You keep your code. It lives on your laptop throughout, so nothing disappears when the session ends.
05

Access, data, and what happens afterwards

  • Your key works until Tuesday 15 September, a week after the session, so you can go back and finish or extend what you built.
  • After that the environment is deleted — keys revoked on 15 September, everything else removed on 22 September. Your local code is unaffected.
  • Want to take it further? If your own team has a Google Cloud project you can deploy to, your key works from there too during that week. Use a secret manager rather than putting it in a container image.
Please read: what not to bring

The lab uses the public SurgVU research dataset only. Do not introduce any patient data, clinical footage, production data or internal intellectual property into the exercise or the shared environment. Nothing built during the session is a medical device or intended for clinical use — it is a training exercise on recorded practice footage.

06

If something does not work

Reply to your credentials email with the entire output of preflight.py — it is written to be pasted straight in, and it usually tells us exactly what is wrong. Ask early rather than close to the deadline: a blocked pip install or a locked-down laptop can take a day or two to resolve on your side, and there is no time on the morning.
